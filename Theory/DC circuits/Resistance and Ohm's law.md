## Resistance in a conductor

Resistance in a conductor depend on its characteristic: 
- Resistance increase with an increase of the length of the conductor
- Resistance increase with a decrees of the cross section of the conductor
- Resistance depend on the characteristic of the metal of the conductor

For any conductor the resistance is describe by the formula:

$$\Large R = ρ \dfrac{l}{S}$$
Where 
- $\Large ρ$ is the resistivity of the metal
- $\Large l$ is the length of the conductor
- $\Large S$ is the cross section of the conductor

#### The effects of temperature on resistance

The resistivity of most material change with changes in temperatures. In general the resistivity of most metal increase with temperatures, while conductor like graphite see a decrease in resistivity with an increase in temperature.

The temperature coefficient that describe these changes in resistivity is generally represented by the Greek letter $\Large α$, this value is equivalent to the change in resistance of a 1 Ohm conductor for a 1°C (or Kelvin) increase in its temperature.

The relationship between temperature and resistivity is described by the following equation:

$$\Large ρ_1 = ρ_0[1 + α(T_1 - T_0)]$$

Where
- $\Large ρ_1$ is the resistivity at temperature $\Large T_1$
- $\Large ρ_0$ is the resistivity at temperature $\Large T_0$
- $\Large α$ is the resistivity temperature coefficient

A similar relationship can be expressed for the resistance:

$$\Large R_1 = R_0[1 + α(T_1 - T_0)]$$
## Ohm's law

When current $\Large I$ flow through a conductor of resistance $\Large R$ a voltage drop $\Large V$ is generated at its ends.

This rule is called Ohm's law and it can be express with the following equation:

$$\Large V = R \cdot I$$

This relation ship can be used to determine the current flowing through a conductor given its resistance and voltage drop or its resistance given the voltage drop and the flowing current:
$$\Large I = \dfrac{V}{R} \hspace{5em} R = \dfrac{V}{I}$$
Where
- $\Large V$ is the voltage drop across the conductor expressed in Volts
- $\Large I$ is the current flowing through the conductor expressed in Ampere
- $\Large R$ is the resistance of the conductor expressed in Ohms
